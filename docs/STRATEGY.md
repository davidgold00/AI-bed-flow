## 1. AI tool architecture

### Tools and why they fit an ED and a small team (rubric 1.3)

| Tool | What it does | Why it fits | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read-only feeds from the EHR and registration system (HL7 ADT events, FHIR notes and vitals) | Bed status, admissions, discharges, transfers, triage data and morning notes | The brief says both systems can export data and connect to outside tools; staff enter nothing twice and the tool writes nothing to the chart | Interface build and vendor fees (budget) |
| Admission model: logistic regression on 13 triage-time fields | Chance of admission and the likely unit at the end of triage; the top three reasons | Explainable, retrains in minutes, test AUC 0.86 on synthetic data | Open source, $0 licence |
| Discharge model: gradient boosting on 13 fields known at rounds | Chance of going home in 24 h at 07:00 rounds | Test AUC 0.85 on synthetic data; reasons shown | Open source, $0 licence |
| AI note reader: Llama 3.2 3B open weights through Ollama, with a rule-based fallback | Finds the open discharge task in the note and quotes the evidence | Runs on Halvian's own server, so no patient text leaves the network and there are no per-call fees; the rules answer if it is down | $0 licence; a small GPU server |
| Bed forecast: statistical tables of arrivals, discharges and cleaning time | Usable beds per unit in 4, 8 and 24 hours with an 80% range | Transparent and cheap to maintain | Open source |
| Dashboard: Streamlit web page on the hospital network | Bed board, flags, rounds list, forecast, approve or override, decision log, fairness check | No install on clinical PCs; one data scientist can maintain it in Python | Open source |

Why both an AI reader and rules: on the templated synthetic notes, the rules were written for the same templates, so they are hard to beat. On wording the rules were not written for, the AI reader scored F1 0.70 against the rules' 0.12 (recall 0.55 against 0.06), which is the argument for a language model on real free-text notes (still to be tested on real, de-identified notes in the pilot). The stricter checks that remove false alarms also cost recall on new wording: the same model answers under the first checks found 78% at 73% precision. Building the check vocabulary from real notes is a pilot task; nothing was tuned on the stress set. The app shows both readers' findings, tagged with which one found each, and the rules always answer if the model is down.

### Technical design: data, flow, where it runs (rubric 1.4)

| Step | Data in | When | Output | Who acts on it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ED admission flags | Age, vital signs, triage level, complaint, arrival mode, ED visits in the past year (never insurance) | Scores refresh every 15 min; bed requests batched every 60 min per unit | Likely admissions, likely unit, reasons, a drafted bed request | Bed manager approves or overrides |
| Discharge readiness | Length of stay so far, mobility, lab trend, diagnosis group, unit, how the patient was admitted, day of week, and the barriers read from the morning note | 07:00 rounds | Chance of going home in 24 h, the barrier quoted from the note, its owner | Unit team at rounds; the case manager works the barrier |
| Bed forecast | Bed status, flags, the rounds list, cleaning times | Every hour | Usable beds per unit at 4, 8 and 24 h with an 80% range | Charge nurse confirms or corrects |
| Oversight | The decision log, outcomes by payer group | Monthly, and on any pause trigger | Accuracy, fairness, override rates, gap table | Tool owner; AI oversight committee |

- **Where it runs.** One hospital-hosted server inside Halvian's network (in the pilot), with a standby server from Phase 2. Staff open it in a browser with their single sign-on. It reads copies of EHR data and writes nothing back to the chart; people record decisions in the EHR as today.
- **What it requires.** Read-only interfaces from the EHR and registration system, a server with a small GPU, one data scientist (0.5 to 1.0 FTE) and a clinical informatics nurse as tool owner. Cost: $610,956 over 18 months, then $210,252 a year (section 3).
- **Scalability.** The models are small; adding a unit means adding its feeds and training its staff. The local model reads a morning's 282 notes in about 3 minutes at the measured 0.57 s per note on a laptop; a server GPU is faster.
- **Reliability.** If a feed is down for more than 30 minutes, units return to phone calls and the whiteboard with a bed huddle every 2 hours. If the language model is down, the rules read the notes and the screen says so. The standby server takes over if the main one fails.
- **Maintenance.** Monthly accuracy, fairness and override checks; a quarterly retraining check; a yearly bias audit and security assessment; each change to a model, the prompt or the note checks gets a new version, and is tested in silent mode first.
- **Privacy.** Minimum necessary fields; insurance is never a model input (used only to audit fairness); no patient data leaves the network; role-based access, encrypted disks and an audit log.
- **Clinical risk.** Nothing executes automatically. Every recommendation shows its reasons and needs a person to approve or override it; admission and discharge decisions stay with clinicians.

### Month 18 goals (rubric 2.1)

Each target is the brief's baseline moved by 75% of the change the paired simulation shows in the Cautious scenario, because real adoption is messier than a simulation, rounded to the brief's own precision.

| G2 Fewer patients leave before being seen | Share of arrivals who leave before a provider sees them | 4.0% | 3.6% | -14% to -8% |
| G3 Narrow the gaps for Medicaid/CHIP and Uninsured patients | Medicaid/CHIP minus Commercial, median minutes to a provider | 10 min | 9 min | Gap narrowed |
| G3 Narrow the gaps for Medicaid/CHIP and Uninsured patients | Uninsured minus Commercial, share leaving before being seen | 2.8 pp | 2.6 pp | Gap narrowed |
| G4 Staff use the tool and still think for themselves | Flags acted on (approved or worked) | new measure | 50% | measured in the pilot |
| G4 Staff use the tool and still think for themselves | Override rate (healthy band) | new measure | 2% to 40% | measured in the pilot |
| G4 Staff use the tool and still think for themselves | Overrides with a recorded reason | new measure | 90% | measured in the pilot |

How we track each goal and what we do if it slips:

- **G1.** Track: Monthly median from the EHR: admit decision to leaving the ED room, all admissions. If off track: Two months off track: review the decision log for unworked flags and slow bed turnover; if under 40% of flags are acted on, run unit huddles and retrain; if flags are acted on but beds still open late, move discharge work earlier at rounds.
- **G2.** Track: Monthly share of arrivals who leave before a provider sees them, from registration and EHR times. If off track: Two months off track: check whether freed rooms are reaching the waiting room (room turnaround), and escalate to the ED operations lead.
- **G3.** Track: Monthly gap table by payer group (payer is used only to audit, never to predict). If off track: A gap that widens past the guardrail pauses the part of the tool involved for review by the oversight committee; a gap that stays flat for two quarters triggers the Phase 2 threshold review and targeted outreach with registration and social work.
- **G4.** Track: The decision log: every approve and override with the person, time and reason. If off track: Under 2% overrides suggests rubber-stamping: audit a sample of approvals with the unit lead. Over 40% suggests mistrust or a model problem: review reasons and recalibrate.
- **Equity guardrail.** No payer gap may widen by more than 2 min or 0.3 pp for 2 months in a row.

Adoption plan for skeptical clinicians and patients (rubric 2.2):

- **Clinicians design it with us.** A charge nurse, a case manager, a hospitalist and a bed manager sit on the pilot team from Month 1 and approve the screens, the wording of every reason and the override reasons.
- **Silent mode first.** Staff see how often the tool would have been right on their own patients before it shows them anything; the results are shared at unit meetings, misses included.
- **Nothing happens without a person.** Every recommendation shows its reasons, the evidence quoted from the note, and an approve or override control. The tool never places an order, moves a patient or contacts a family.
- **Overrides are welcome.** Each override reason goes to the weekly huddle; the most common reasons change the tool, and staff see the change.
- **Super-users on every shift** answer questions at the bedside, and unit leaders report adoption and overrides openly.
- **Patients hear it from people.** The patient and family advisory council reviews how staff explain the tool. Patients are told that a nurse or doctor makes every decision, and that their insurance is never used to rank them.

Published evidence:

- [Johns Hopkins study of discharge predictions in multidisciplinary rounds](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/machine-learning-based-hospital-discharge-predictions-can-support/): Feeding machine learning discharge predictions into daily rounds was followed by stays more than 12 hours shorter on 2 of 4 units (a medicine and a telemetry unit), with no change on the other two.

## 4. Risk assessment and mitigation

### Risks and why they matter (rubric 4.1)

| Risk | Why it matters here | What we do | Where it shows in the demo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Algorithmic bias | The brief warns that tools have failed patients who were already underserved. A model trained on past flow can favor groups who already move fastest. | Payer is never a model input. A pre-launch bias test separates case mix from model behavior. Monthly gap tables by payer with a guardrail that pauses the tool. | Oversight: Fairness check, Waits and walkouts by group, When to pause |
| Patient privacy | Notes and triage data are protected health information. | Everything runs inside Halvian's network; the language model is local, with no cloud API. Read-only feeds, role-based access, an audit log. | Note reader status line: running locally |
| Over-reliance on AI | Staff can defer to a confident screen, and a wrong discharge or bed decision harms patients. | Nothing executes automatically. Every recommendation shows its reasons and needs an approve or override; overrides need a reason; AI-only note findings say "check the note"; an override rate under 2% triggers an audit of approvals. | Approve or override on every page, decision log |
| Loss of human connection | Faster flow can turn discharge into a transaction, and patients may feel processed by a machine. | The tool removes chart-hunting and phone calls, not conversations: case managers spend the time saved with patients and families, discharge teaching stays face to face, and the tool never contacts patients. The patient advisory council reviews what patients are told. | Discharge Readiness: the owner is a person, not the tool |

### Data security and the response plan (rubric 4.2)

Protection: the tool reads copies of EHR data through read-only interfaces and writes nothing back to the
chart. It runs on hospital-hosted servers inside the network, with encrypted disks, single sign-on with
role-based access, and an audit log of every view and decision. The language model is open-weight and local,
so no patient text leaves Halvian. Vendors get no standing access; the third-party assessment and yearly
re-test are in the budget.

If Halvian's or a vendor's systems are compromised:

1. **Contain (first hour).** The tool owner and IT security cut the tool's interfaces and take it offline.
   Units switch to the downtime protocol below, so care never waits on the tool.
2. **Assess (first day).** Security determines what data was reachable. The tool keeps no data beyond what the
   EHR already holds, which limits exposure.
3. **Notify.** The privacy officer follows HIPAA breach notification rules: affected patients and regulators
   within 60 days, sooner if state law requires.
4. **Recover.** Rebuild from clean images and the versioned model files, rotate credentials, and run silent
   mode for a week before staff see recommendations again.
5. **Review.** The oversight committee reviews the incident and the vendor's response, and keeps or retires
   the vendor.

### Oversight and contingency plans (rubric 4.3)

- **Before launch:** silent mode on Halvian's own data and the bias test above, which must pass the pilot gate.
- **After launch:** monthly checks against the pause conditions (AUC, calibration drift, miss-rate gaps,
  override rate band, payer gaps, feed outages), owned by the tool owner and reviewed by the oversight
  committee.
- **When to pause or retire:** a payer gap widening past the guardrail for two months, AUC below 0.75, an
  override rate outside 2% to 40%, or a safety event pauses the tool; missing goals for two quarters after a
  recovery plan retires it.
- **Review without deferring:** reasons are shown before the recommendation, overrides need a reason, approvals
  are audited when overrides fall under 2%, and AI-only note findings are marked for checking.
- **Contingency:** if feeds are down for more than 30 minutes, units return to phone calls and the whiteboard
  with a bed huddle every 2 hours. If the language model is down, the note reader falls back to the rules and
  says so. A standby server takes over if the main one fails. Downtime drills run on every unit in Phase 2.
